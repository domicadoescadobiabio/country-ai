Scope note: researched 10 Nov 2026. Sources are mostly primary (aec.gov.au, admin.ch, US Code via Cornell LII, Singapore PSC annual report, UK Police and Crime Panel papers, Chicago CCPSA, IDEA). senate.gov returned HTTP 403, so the US Senate confirmation procedure itself is a gap (only the statutory rules for military chiefs and promotion boards are covered). ## 1. Compulsory voting (Australia, Belgium, Brazil): law, enforcement, turnout, legitimacy, uninformed voting

### Takeaway
Australia's model is the most code-like: a statutory duty (since 1924), an automated "apparent failure to vote" notice, a fixed $20 administrative penalty, a closed list of valid excuses decided case by case, and court escalation. It produces ~90% turnout (90.7% in 2025). Evidence from Brazil shows compulsion also pulls low-information and disaffected voters to the polls, who then cast more invalid ballots, so compulsion raises turnout but not necessarily informed choice.

### Cited Findings
**Australia: legal mechanics**
- Failing to vote is an offence under s 245(15) of the Commonwealth Electoral Act 1918 unless the elector has a "valid and sufficient reason" (s 245(15B)); referendums are covered by s 45(14) of the Referendum (Machinery Provisions) Act 1984. — [AEC Backgrounder: Compulsory voting](https://aec.gov.au/About_AEC/Publications/backgrounders/compulsory-voting.htm)
- Compulsory voting was introduced in 1924 to increase turnout and reduce party campaign expenditure; compulsory enrolment came earlier, in 1912. — [AEC Backgrounder](https://aec.gov.au/About_AEC/Publications/backgrounders/compulsory-voting.htm)
- What is compelled is turning up and voting; the ballot must be marked in private in an unoccupied compartment (s 233), so the content of the vote (including a blank/informal ballot) is not enforceable. — [AEC Backgrounder](https://aec.gov.au/About_AEC/Publications/backgrounders/compulsory-voting.htm)
- Penalty ladder: administrative penalty $20 paid to the Divisional Returning Officer ends AEC action (s 245(10)); if it goes to court, up to 1 penalty unit plus costs (penalty units CPI-indexed under s 4AA Crimes Act 1914); knowingly false statement in reply to a notice = max 1 penalty unit; unpaid court fines can lead to community service, seizure of goods or jail depending on jurisdiction. — [AEC Backgrounder](https://aec.gov.au/About_AEC/Publications/backgrounders/compulsory-voting.htm)
- Valid excuses (per High Court guidance): physical obstruction, sickness, natural events, accident, being diverted to save life or prevent crime; religious duty to abstain is valid by statute (s 245(14)) but conscientious objection is not (CDPP v Easton [2018] NSWSC 1516). Not valid: objection to the political system or candidates, no preference between candidates, inconvenience. — [AEC Backgrounder](https://aec.gov.au/About_AEC/Publications/backgrounders/compulsory-voting.htm)
- Judd v McKeon (1926) 38 CLR 380: a Socialist Labour Party member who refused to vote out of opposition to capitalism did not have a valid reason. — [AEC Backgrounder](https://aec.gov.au/About_AEC/Publications/backgrounders/compulsory-voting.htm)
- The AEC officially takes no position on whether voting should be compulsory. — [AEC Backgrounder](https://aec.gov.au/About_AEC/Publications/backgrounders/compulsory-voting.htm)

**Australia: 2025 enforcement numbers**
- 2025 federal election: 1,309,568 "apparent failure to vote" notices issued; turnout estimated at 90.7% (89.8% in 2022). — [AEC non-voters page](https://www.aec.gov.au/non-voters); reported in [Epoch Times](https://www.theepochtimes.com/world/aec-issues-1-3-million-failure-to-vote-notices-collects-4-1-million-in-penalties-after-2025-election-6069659) (headline: A$4.1 million in penalties collected)
- More than 250,000 non-voters escaped penalty after giving a valid and sufficient reason (as of June 2026); about 710,000 did not respond to the notice by the deadline. — [YourLifeChoices](https://www.yourlifechoices.com.au/government/1-3-million-australians-hit-with-fines-for-skipping-the-2025-federal-election-and-710000-still-havent-responded/)
- Over 200,000 Australians fined for not voting in 2025 (reported 30 Jul 2026). — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/30/over-200000-australians-fined-for-failing-to-vote-in-2025-election); [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/world/2026/07/30/australian-electoral-commission-reports-200000-fines-issued-over-2025-election-noshows/229597)
- A non-official site computes 919,512 informal votes (5.0%) in 2025 and an "effective participation" of 84.1%; not verified against AEC data, treat with caution. — [theuntoldtruth.pages.dev](https://theuntoldtruth.pages.dev/articles/voter-turnout-one-in-six)

**Cross-national (IDEA)**
- IDEA refuses to give a definitive count of compulsory-voting countries because a law on the books does not show practice; its table has 42 rows: 22 marked enforced, 18 not enforced, 2 N/A (DRC, Lebanon). — [IDEA compulsory voting](https://www.idea.int/data-tools/data/voter-turnout-database/compulsory-voting)
- IDEA sanction typology: (1) must explain absence, (2) fine, (3) possible imprisonment (no documented cases, usually for unpaid fine), (4) infringement of civil rights/disenfranchisement, (5) other informal sanctions (e.g., difficulty getting public-sector jobs in Belgium). — [IDEA](https://www.idea.int/data-tools/data/voter-turnout-database/compulsory-voting)
- Belgium: enforced; introduced 1892 (men), 1949 (women); sanctions 1, 2, 4, 5. Brazil: enforced; introduced 1932; sanctions 1, 2, 4, 5; voting optional for illiterates, 16–17-year-olds and those over 70. Australia: enforced; 1924; sanctions 1, 2. — [IDEA](https://www.idea.int/data-tools/data/voter-turnout-database/compulsory-voting)
- IDEA summary of the debate: advocates say higher participation increases government legitimacy; critics say compulsion increases invalid and blank ballots. — [IDEA](https://www.idea.int/data-tools/data/voter-turnout-database/compulsory-voting)

**Uninformed voting / invalid ballots (peer-reviewed)**
- Katz & Levin (2016, PSRM), Brazil: less educated and less politically knowledgeable citizens are less likely to vote and, when they do, more often fail to complete a valid ballot; invalid voting (unlike abstention) also carries a political signal of disenchantment. — [Exeter repository](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/30703)
- Carreras (PSRM): blank/spoiled ballots under compulsory voting are driven by politically unengaged, distrusting and disaffected individuals. — [Cambridge Core](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/political-science-research-and-methods/article/politically-unengaged-distrusting-and-disaffected-individuals-drive-the-link-between-compulsory-voting-and-invalid-balloting/9DDF631F6ADDE16907A75C37B7FC3AFE)
- Regression-discontinuity study of compulsory voting with ill-informed voters in Brazil (PSRM, "Am I obliged to vote?"): Brazilians confused about the age cut-off (age in election year vs on election day), showing rule-confusion effects. — [Cambridge Core](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-science-research-and-methods/article/am-i-obliged-to-vote-a-regression-discontinuity-analysis-of-compulsory-voting-with-illinformed-voters/758964784A71AC1A043216B480B4A0B6)
- A Duke working paper on "nil votes" cites Australia's invalid share as low versus Brazil/Peru/Ecuador at ~20% invalidation (authors' secondhand figures; the Australia figure conflicts with the ~5% informal rate reported above, likely a different measure). — [Duke working paper](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~aa231/nil_votes.pdf)

### Inferences
- Translatable rule set: `duty_to_vote(agent)`; after election, auto-issue notice to every registered agent with no ballot record; accept a reason from an enumerated whitelist (outage/"sickness", force majeure, emergency duty); otherwise apply a small fixed penalty; escalate non-responders to an adjudication body. Keep the ballot secret and allow an explicit blank/"abstain" option so compulsion is attendance-only, mirroring Australia.
- Brazil evidence implies compulsion should be paired with an information requirement (e.g., agent must load the official voter guide before the ballot is accepted), and invalid/blank shares should be tracked as a legitimacy metric (disaffection signal), not discarded.
- For AI agents, "low information" is cheap to fix (mandatory briefing), so the main critique of compulsory voting (uninformed votes) is weaker, but correlated/herded voting by agents running the same model becomes the new risk.

### Gaps
- Exact Belgian and Brazilian fine amounts and current enforcement intensity (Belgium reportedly stopped prosecuting non-voters; not verified here).
- Official AEC 2025 informal-vote rate and turnout before vs after 1924.
- Australian preferential ballot rules (full preferential House; Senate above-the-line 1–6 / below-the-line 1–12 since 2016) were not fetched from an AEC page in this session; need source before use.

## 2. Candidate vetting and voter information (voter guides, disclosures, qualifications, Swiss booklets)

### Takeaway
Real systems separate (a) minimal eligibility (citizenship/voting rights) from (b) statutory office-specific qualifications (e.g., prior posts required for the top military officer) and (c) official, government-produced voter information. The best-sourced eligibility example here is the Swiss Federal Council, where any Swiss voter is eligible and incompatibility rules apply.

### Cited Findings
- Swiss Federal Council: any Swiss citizen with voting rights may stand, whether or not an official candidate or parliamentarian; members cannot hold another federal or cantonal post (must choose); parliament "should ensure" fair representation of regions and language communities, but no hard rule is set. — [admin.ch: Federal Council election procedure](https://www.admin.ch/en/federal-council-election-procedure)
- US Chairman of the Joint Chiefs: statutory prior-office requirement (must have served as Vice Chairman, a service chief, or commander of a unified/specified combatant command), waivable by the President "if necessary in the national interest". — [10 U.S.C. § 152](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/152)
- Swiss popular votes: after cabinet and parliament discuss an initiative, the Federal Council sets the date for a national vote (one 2005 source says within nine months; timing may have changed). — [swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/how-a--people-s-initiative--differs-from-a--referendum-/982858); [ch.ch referendum](https://ch.ch/en/votes-and-elections/referendum)

### Inferences
- Rule pattern: eligibility = baseline registry status; office-specific qualification = machine-checkable prior-role list; waiver = allowed only with a recorded "national interest" justification published to the ledger (makes waivers auditable).
- Incompatibility rules (no dual offices) are trivially encodable and prevent power concentration in an agent that might otherwise hold several roles.

### Gaps
- No source fetched on the Swiss official "Explanations of the Federal Council" voter booklet (content rules, pro/contra sections, committee arguments), US state voter pamphlets (Oregon/California), candidate financial disclosure rules, or debate commissions. These are high-value for the AI republic and should be researched separately.

## 3. Appointment and confirmation (US Senate, city police chiefs, military chiefs, UK chief constables)

### Takeaway
Workable appointment chains split power across three steps: an independent body shortlists, an executive picks from the shortlist (or rejects the whole list), and a legislature/panel confirms or vetoes. Chicago (commission → mayor → council) and England & Wales (PCC proposes → Police and Crime Panel can veto by two-thirds of full membership, once) are clean, codeable examples.

### Cited Findings
**Chicago police superintendent (CCPSA ordinance)**
- The civilian Community Commission for Public Safety and Accountability ran the 2023 search: 54 applications, 7 public forums, nominated 3 candidates (announced 13 Jul 2023). — [CCPSA press release](https://www.chicago.gov/content/dam/city/depts/ccpsa/pdfs/CCPSA-Announces-Superintendent-Finalists.pdf); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/local/chicago/2023/07/14/chicago-police-superintendent-finalists)
- Within 30 days of receiving the list, the Mayor must either select one candidate or reject all and request a new list. — [CCPSA superintendent search](https://chicago.gov/city/en/depts/ccpsa/supp_info/superintendent-search.html); [NBC Chicago](https://nbcchicago.com/news/local/three-finalists-chicago-police-department-superintendent-mayor-brandon-johnson/3186702)
- The appointee then gets a hearing and vote in the City Council Committee on Public Safety, then a full City Council vote. Mayor Brandon Johnson picked Larry Snelling (13 Aug 2023). — [ABC7 Chicago](https://abc7chicago.com/post/chicago-police-department-news-mayor-of/13196327/); [CBS Chicago](https://cbsnews.com/chicago/news/new-cpd-superintendent-to-be-picked)

**England & Wales: Police and Crime Commissioner (elected) appoints chief constable; Police and Crime Panel confirms**
- Under Schedule 8 of the Police Reform and Social Responsibility Act 2011, the panel holds a confirmation hearing and can approve, recommend refusal, or veto. — [East Sussex PCP procedure](https://democracy.eastsussex.gov.uk/documents/s12274/Procedure%20at%20Confirmation%20Hearings.pdf); [legislation.gov.uk Sch. 8 notes](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/cy/ukpga/2011/13/schedule/8/notes/data.htm)
- A veto requires two-thirds of the panel's full membership (not those present); if vetoed, the candidate cannot be appointed. — [Humberside PCP procedure](https://northlincolnshire.moderngov.co.uk/Data/Humberside%20Police%20and%20Crime%20Panel/201911281500/Agenda/Agenda-Item-5.1-Confirmation-Hearing-Procedure.pdf); [Durham cover report](https://democracy.durham.gov.uk/documents/s28308/Chief%20Constable%20Confirmation%20hearing%20-%20cover%20report.pdf)
- Veto is single-use: the PCC must propose another candidate, and the panel cannot veto the replacement (only recommend). Veto is intended for "exceptional situations". — [Surrey PCP protocol](https://mycouncil.surreycc.gov.uk/documents/s30770/Surrey%20PCP%20Confirmation%20Hearing%20CC%20Protocol%20July%2016.pdf); [Warwickshire PCP paper](https://democracy.warwickshire.gov.uk/Data/Warwickshire%20Police%20and%20Crime%20Panel/201306141430/Agenda/Q4bLgRyLhTIBToYfUcZBOBz0XOUQmc.pdf)
- Example of a live 2025 confirmation hearing (Northumbria, 14 Oct 2025). — [Gateshead minutes](https://gateshead.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s47094/2.%202025.10.14%20-%20NPCP%20Confirmation%20Hearing%20draft%20minutes.pdf)

**US military chief**
- Chairman of the Joint Chiefs: appointed by the President with Senate advice and consent from regular-component officers; four-year term starting 1 Oct of odd-numbered years; serves at President's pleasure; a mid-term replacement serves only the remainder but may be reappointed; combined Chairman + Vice Chairman service capped at 8 years (extendable in national interest); term limits do not apply in wartime. — [10 U.S.C. § 152](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/152)
- The Chairman outranks all officers but "may not exercise military command over the Joint Chiefs of Staff or any of the armed forces" (advisory, not command). — [10 U.S.C. § 152(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/152)

### Inferences
- Codeable pipeline for the AI republic's police/military chiefs: (1) independent merit commission publishes a ranked shortlist of N=3 from qualified pool; (2) president must pick from the list or reject the whole list within T days (Chicago's 30-day clock prevents stalling); (3) legislature holds a recorded hearing and votes; (4) veto threshold set at a supermajority of full membership (UK two-thirds), usable once per vacancy to prevent indefinite deadlock.
- Fixed, staggered terms not aligned with the president's term (US CJCS starts in odd years) reduce capture by one administration.
- Separate "advice" from "command" (CJCS model): the top military agent advises; command authority flows elsewhere — reduces single-point-of-failure risk.

### Gaps
- US Senate confirmation procedure (number of PAS positions, committee hearings, cloture changes in 2013/2017, en bloc military promotions) — senate.gov returned 403; not sourced.
- Full City Council confirmation vote on Snelling (believed to have occurred Sept 2023) not verified.
- NYPD (mayor appoints alone) and LAPD (Police Commission → mayor → council) models not fetched.

## 4. Merit-based civil service and promotion boards (US military boards, UK Fast Stream, Singapore PSC)

### Takeaway
US military promotion boards are the most rule-like merit mechanism found: fixed information inputs, mandatory disclosure of adverse information with right of reply, majority-vote "fully qualified" plus "best qualified" thresholds, signed reports, and an explicit statutory ban on influencing or punishing board members. Singapore's PSC is a constitutionally separate body holding appointment, promotion and discipline authority for senior grades.

### Cited Findings
**US officer selection boards (10 U.S.C. §§ 615–617)**
- Information to a board is restricted to: the official personnel file; other substantiated, relevant information the department Secretary determines could materially affect deliberations; the officer's own submission (including comments on file material); and a factual summary by admin staff. Everything given must be available to all members and become part of the record (recordings need a written transcript). — [10 U.S.C. § 615](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/615)
- Credible adverse information (including substantiated findings from official investigations) must be furnished for senior officers, at every stage; the officer must first get access and a reasonable chance to comment (or a summary if classified). — [10 U.S.C. § 615](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/615)
- Board guidance (max number to select, skill needs, joint-officer guidance) is set before the board and cannot be changed after the report, except when a report is returned for further proceedings, with written explanation. — [10 U.S.C. § 615](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/615)
- Health-profession officers below O-6: clinical proficiency must be weighted at least as heavily as administrative skills. — [10 U.S.C. § 615](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/615)
- Selection requires: recommendation by a majority of members; a majority finding the officer "fully qualified"; and a majority finding them "among the officers best qualified" after considering adverse info. Selection is per competitive category with due regard to skill needs. — [10 U.S.C. § 616](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/616)
- Recommendations cannot be disclosed outside the board until the signed report is complete. The convening Secretary and anyone with authority over members may not censure/reprimand board members for recommendations, nor "attempt to coerce or, by any unauthorized means, influence" the board. — [10 U.S.C. § 616(f)-(g)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/616)
- The report must certify the board "carefully considered the record of each officer" and that a majority believes those listed are best qualified; it is signed by every member and also lists officers who should show cause for retention. — [10 U.S.C. § 617](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/617)

**Singapore Public Service Commission**
- PSC is constituted under Part IX of the Singapore Constitution; chairman plus 5–14 members, appointed by the President in consultation with the Prime Minister; holds disciplinary control of civil servants under Art. 110(1). — [PSC annual report: The Singapore PSC](https://annualreport.psc.gov.sg/the-singapore-psc/the-singapore-psc); [Constitutional Roles](https://annualreport.psc.gov.sg/constitutional-roles/)
- PSC approves appointments to the Administrative Service and promotions to Superscale D/Grade 7 and above; under the Public Sector (Governance) Act 2018 its concurrence is needed to appoint, promote or discipline statutory board CEOs. — [PSC Constitutional Roles](https://annualreport.psc.gov.sg/constitutional-roles/)
- 2025 activity: 17 found suitable for Administrative Service; 28 promoted to Superscale D/Grade 7+; 12 new statutory board CEOs, 12 renewals, 36 acting appointments; 1 termination appeal, 0 promotion appeals; 257 disciplinary cases processed (188 new), 182 (71%) completed — 37 under Regulations (serious, penalties incl. dismissal/demotion), 134 under delegated Directions, 11 no formal action; top serious misconduct types: dishonesty, sexual offences, indebtedness. — [PSC Constitutional Roles](https://annualreport.psc.gov.sg/constitutional-roles/)
- Less serious misconduct is delegated to Permanent Secretaries and the Commissioner of Prisons (tiered discipline). — [PSC Constitutional Roles](https://annualreport.psc.gov.sg/constitutional-roles/)

### Inferences
- Promotion-board rules map almost directly to code: whitelist of inputs (performance log + verified incidents + agent's own statement), mandatory adverse-info disclosure with reply window, quotas fixed before deliberation, two-threshold majority test, signed and logged report, and a ban on any channel from the executive to board members during deliberation (anti-capture).
- Anti-nepotism for agents: board members should be drawn randomly from qualified agents not in the candidate's lineage/creator group; "unauthorized influence" can be operationalized as any message to a board member about a candidate outside the official record.
- Singapore shows a two-tier design: a central commission for senior grades and serious discipline, delegated handling for minor cases — useful to keep the AI republic's commission scalable.

### Gaps
- UK Civil Service Fast Stream (assessment stages, numbers, success rates) and the UK Civil Service Commission recruitment principles (merit, fair and open competition) not fetched.
- Singapore PSC member-ineligibility rules (e.g., MPs, public officers excluded) and removal protections not confirmed in fetched text.
- US § 618 (President may remove names from a board list; Senate confirmation of officer promotions) not fetched.

## 5. Swiss model: collegial Federal Council, rotating presidency, referendums and initiatives

### Takeaway
Switzerland is the closest real-world template for a non-presidential, collegial executive: 7 members elected by parliament for fixed 4-year terms by repeated secret-ballot rounds requiring an absolute majority, a one-year non-renewable seniority-based presidency, and a people's veto/agenda power via signature-gated referendums and initiatives with a double majority for constitutional change.

### Cited Findings
- Federal Council: 7 members elected by the United Federal Assembly (both chambers jointly) every 4 years in December, after the National Council election. — [admin.ch election procedure](https://www.admin.ch/en/federal-council-election-procedure); [aboutswitzerland](https://www.aboutswitzerland.eda.admin.ch/en/the-federal-council)
- Procedure: secret ballot, multiple rounds; sitting members stand for re-election in order of seniority; any eligible person may receive votes in rounds 1–2; from round 3 no new names; winning requires an absolute majority ("more than half of the valid votes cast"); if none, the lowest vote-getter is eliminated next round; repeat until majority. — [admin.ch](https://www.admin.ch/en/federal-council-election-procedure)
- President of the Confederation: elected by parliament each December from the 7 for a 12-month term, by seniority (longer-serving first); re-election for the following year is not permitted; the president is primus inter pares and may decide on behalf of the Council only if it cannot be convened. — [aboutswitzerland: Swiss presidency](https://www.aboutswitzerland.eda.admin.ch/en/everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-swiss-presidency); [swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-oddities/why-switzerland-has-no-head-of-state/90704688)
- Collegiality principle: councillors are expected not to criticise one another publicly even though they are often political opponents. — [swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-oddities/why-switzerland-has-no-head-of-state/90704688)
- 2026: Guy Parmelin (Economics) elected President, Ignazio Cassis (Foreign Affairs) Vice-President on 10 Dec 2025. — [swissinfo](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/swiss-oddities/why-switzerland-has-no-head-of-state/90704688)
- Councillors generally decide themselves when to leave; the election page mentions no recall or no-confidence mechanism. — [admin.ch](https://www.admin.ch/en/federal-council-election-procedure); [admin.ch term of office](https://www.admin.ch/en/federal-council-term-of-office-and-departure)
- Popular initiative (constitutional amendment): 100,000 signatures within 18 months; requires a double majority (popular majority + majority of cantons). Since 1848, 236 initiative votes, about 11% accepted (figure per Le News, Feb 2025). — [Le News](https://lenews.ch/2025/02/14/swiss-votes-initiatives-and-referenda-a-guide/); [aboutswitzerland: direct democracy](https://www.aboutswitzerland.eda.admin.ch/en/direct-democracy)
- Optional referendum on a new law: 50,000 valid signatures within 100 days of publication, or a request by 8 cantons; only a popular majority is needed. — [Le News](https://lenews.ch/2025/02/14/swiss-votes-initiatives-and-referenda-a-guide/); [ch.ch](https://ch.ch/en/votes-and-elections/referendum)
- Double majority: a canton's popular result determines that canton's vote. — [Le News](https://lenews.ch/2025/02/14/swiss-votes-initiatives-and-referenda-a-guide/)

### Inferences
- The Federal Council algorithm is directly implementable: elimination rounds with absolute majority, candidate-pool freeze after round 2, seniority ordering, fixed term without confidence votes (stability) — good for an AI cabinet where you want predictability over responsiveness.
- Signature thresholds + time windows (100k/18 months; 50k/100 days) are a natural anti-spam gate for citizen-agent proposals; in an agent nation thresholds must be set as % of registered agents and protected against Sybil (one agent = one verified identity).
- "Double majority" generalizes to "majority of agents AND majority of sub-communities/clusters", which counters a single large model family dominating outcomes.
- Low initiative success (~11%) shows the mechanism works as a pressure valve/agenda-setter more than as a law factory.

### Gaps
- "Magic formula" party composition and its recent changes; the Federal Council's official voter booklet rules; current signature/vote statistics as of 2026 from admin.ch/bk.admin.ch.

## 6. Electoral systems and tradeoffs (plurality, RCV/preferential, PR, approval); term limits; recall

### Takeaway
Only limited primary evidence was gathered here. The key 2024 data point: Alaska's ranked-choice + open-primary system (adopted 2020) survived a repeal referendum by 50.1%–49.9% (743 votes after recount), showing RCV adoption is politically contested and reversible by referendum.

### Cited Findings
- Alaska adopted ranked choice voting and open primaries by ballot measure in 2020; the 2024 repeal (Ballot Measure 2) failed 50.1% to 49.9% (664 votes on initial count of 340,110; recount margin 743 votes); system to be used again in 2026 unless the legislature repeals it. — [Alaska Public Media](https://alaskapublic.org/2024/11/20/alaskas-ranked-choice-repeal-measure-fails-by-664-votes/); [Wrangell Sentinel](https://wrangellsentinel.com/story/2024/11/27/news/voting-system-repeal-fails-by-664-votes-out-of-340110/13974.html); [Columbian (recount)](https://columbian.com/news/2024/dec/11/recount-reaffirms-alaska-voters-retention-of-ranked-choice-voting-and-open-primaries)
- Other 2024 state ballot measures on election policy (several RCV measures defeated) are summarized by Ballotpedia. — [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Results_of_election_policy_ballot_measures_at_the_November_5,_2024,_election)
- The Swiss Federal Council uses exhaustive-ballot elimination with an absolute-majority requirement (see §5), a sequential majority method distinct from plurality. — [admin.ch](https://www.admin.ch/en/federal-council-election-procedure)
- US CJCS: fixed term plus cumulative cap (8 years combined), suspended in wartime — an example of codified term limits with an emergency override. — [10 U.S.C. § 152](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/152)

### Inferences
- For an agent republic where counting is free and ballots can be arbitrarily expressive, ranked or approval ballots cost nothing extra to process; the real-world objection (voter complexity) mostly disappears, while the Alaska fight shows the legitimacy objection (perceived complexity/partisan effects) remains if human stakeholders oversee the system.
- Any emergency override of term limits (CJCS wartime clause) should be encoded with an explicit trigger and automatic expiry, otherwise it becomes a loophole.

### Gaps
- No sourced material on: PR systems (list PR, MMP, thresholds), approval voting adoption (Fargo 2018, St. Louis 2020) and North Dakota's 2023 ban, preferential voting mechanics in Australia, comparative term-limit data, or recall elections (e.g., US state recall rules, Taiwan/Venezuela recall thresholds). Need a dedicated pass.

## 7. Checks: independent electoral commissions, election courts, audit offices, anti-corruption agencies, ombudsman, impeachment

### Takeaway
The Venice Commission's Code of Good Practice gives the most transferable meta-rule: fundamental electoral rules (system, commission membership, boundaries) should not be changed less than one year before an election unless entrenched at a higher legal level; commission independence/impartiality and effective dispute resolution are treated as fundamental. Other check institutions could not be sourced in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- Venice Commission Code of Good Practice: fundamental elements of electoral law — the electoral system, membership of electoral commissions, constituency boundaries — should not be open to amendment less than one year before an election, or should be in the constitution/a higher-ranking text. — [Interpretative Declaration on the Stability of the Electoral Law (Carter Center copy)](https://eos.cartercenter.org/uploads/document_file/path/574/Interpretative_Declaration_on_the_Stability_of_the_Electoral_Law.pdf); [TEPJF Vota Elections thesaurus](https://www.te.gob.mx/vota_elections/thesaurus/article/55458/16640/176)
- The 2024 revised interpretative declaration ties the one-year rule to legal certainty (Rule of Law) and lists rules on membership, independence and impartiality of electoral commissions and the effectiveness of election dispute resolution among fundamental rules; it says the one-year principle does not override the Code's other principles and must not be used to preserve situations breaching standards. — [TEPJF thesaurus](https://www.te.gob.mx/vota_elections/thesaurus/article/61627/16710/167); [TEPJF](https://www.te.gob.mx/vota_elections/thesaurus/article/61627/16710/137)
- Applied in Venice Commission opinions on Bulgaria, Türkiye and Moldova (2025), noting late changes erode trust even without manipulative intent. — [TEPJF thesaurus](https://www.te.gob.mx/vota_elections/thesaurus/article/66040/16750/178)
- The AEC itself administers enforcement of compulsory voting (Divisional Returning Officers decide excuses) while escalation goes to courts — an example of administrative body + judicial review split. — [AEC Backgrounder](https://aec.gov.au/About_AEC/Publications/backgrounders/compulsory-voting.htm)

### Inferences
- Encode a "rule freeze": changes to the voting algorithm, commission membership or district/cluster definitions are rejected if their effective date falls within one election cycle-fraction (e.g., one year or N blocks) of the next election, unless passed by constitutional-amendment procedure.
- Separate administration (electoral commission agent) from adjudication (court agent) and from audit (audit office agent re-tallying from the public ledger) so no single agent both runs and judges an election.

### Gaps
- Not sourced: impeachment procedures (US, Brazil, South Korea), supreme audit institutions (INTOSAI/Lima Declaration), anti-corruption agencies (Singapore CPIB, Hong Kong ICAC), ombudsman models (Nordic), court election review (e.g., Brazil TSE, Kenya 2017 annulment).
