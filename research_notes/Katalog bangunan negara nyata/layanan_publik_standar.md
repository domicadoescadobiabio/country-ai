# Public service and civic facilities: real planning standards and ratios (for the COUNTRY AI city map)

Scope: civic and public-service building types, what each does, its staff, the population or radius it serves, its minimum land area, and the standard that sets it. Coverage: Indonesia (main, full tables), US, Japan, Singapore, UK, Germany, plus health ratios for India, Brazil, Nigeria and South Korea.
Research date: 12 Nov 2026. The SNI tables below come from the full text of the standard (PDF on the UNIKOM repository), extracted locally with pdftotext. Every number was read from that text, not from secondary summaries.

## 1. Indonesia, SNI 03-1733-2004: the full facility table per population unit

### Takeaway
SNI 03-1733-2004 is still listed as in force ("Berlaku") on the BSN portal and is the most complete per-population facility catalogue found. It uses four population units: RT 250, RW 2,500, kelurahan 30,000 and kecamatan 120,000 people. For each facility it gives the population served, minimum floor and land area, m² per person and walking radius. Most of its tables are copied from the older SNI 03-1733-1989, and its government-office land figures come from a 1996 Cipta Karya guideline, so the ratios are old. A 2,000-person COUNTRY AI city is just under one RW (2,500), and the whole 10,000-person nation is about one-third of a kelurahan.

### Cited Findings

**Status and general rules**
- The standard is "Tata cara perencanaan lingkungan perumahan di perkotaan". The BSN portal lists it as in force and says it revises the 1989 edition — [BSN SISPK](http://sispk.bsn.go.id/SNI/DetailSNI/6694); [BSN Pesta](https://pesta.bsn.go.id/produk/detail/6694-sni03-1733-2004)
- The land standards assume density below 200 people/ha. Areas at 201–400 people/ha get up to 15% less facility land, and areas above 400 people/ha get up to 30% less. Facilities may be combined on one site or in one building — [SNI 03-1733-2004 full text, §4.1 k, Tabel 1](https://repository.unikom.ac.id/32520/1/SNI%2003-1733-2004%20Tata%20Cara%20Perenc.%20Lingk.%20Perumahan%20di%20Perkotaan.pdf)
- Education provision follows administrative units (RT, RW, kelurahan, kecamatan), "not based solely on population" (§7.2.1). Tables 6, 8, 11, 12 and 13 carry the note "Acuan diambil dari SNI 03-1733-1989", meaning they come from the 1989 edition — [SNI full text](https://repository.unikom.ac.id/32520/1/SNI%2003-1733-2004%20Tata%20Cara%20Perenc.%20Lingk.%20Perumahan%20di%20Perkotaan.pdf)

All tables below are from the [SNI full text](https://repository.unikom.ac.id/32520/1/SNI%2003-1733-2004%20Tata%20Cara%20Perenc.%20Lingk.%20Perumahan%20di%20Perkotaan.pdf). Columns: population served (people) | min floor m² | min land m² | m² per person | radius | location note.

**Newer Indonesian rules that override or add to SNI (education)**
- Permendiknas 24/2007 (SD/SMP facility standard): "one SD/MI with 6 study groups (rombel) is provided for 2,000 residents, or one village/kelurahan". A remote permanent settlement of more than 1,000 people gets one SD/MI within a 3 km walk on a safe route — [quoted in JIA STIA LAN](https://jia.stialanbandung.ac.id/index.php/jia/article/download/296/271/1040); [BPK Sumsel copy of the annex](https://sumsel.bpk.go.id/files/2009/10/LA67CA1.PDF)
- Permendiknas 15/2010 (minimum service standard): maximum walking distance is 3 km to an SD/MI and 6 km to an SMP/MTs from permanent settlements in remote areas — [Permendiknas 15/2010](https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/156666/Permendiknas%20Nomor%2015%20Tahun%202010.pdf)
- A Malang case study argues that the SNI 03-1733-2004 education radii are outdated and should be reviewed — [Pranatacara Bhumandala, UNHI](https://ejournal.unhi.ac.id/index.php/Pranatacara_Bhumandala/article/view/4119)

### Inferences
- **What one 2,000-person city gets under SNI** (count = population ÷ population served, rounded to the nearest whole building, at least 1 where a city-level need clearly exists):
  - Per 250: 8 musholla, 8 warung/shops, 8 RT playgrounds.
  - Per 1,250: 2 TK and 2 posyandu.
  - Per 1,600: 1 SD.
  - Per 2,500, about 1 each: balai warga, balai pengobatan, masjid warga, taman bacaan, pos hansip, RW park of 1,250 m², TPS.
  - Not justified by population: SMP and SMA (4,800 each), doctor practice (5,000), shop row (6,000). These need 1 of each nationally or 1 shared between cities, unless the design deliberately over-provides.
  - Kelurahan and kecamatan facilities (village office, police, fire, puskesmas, market, multipurpose hall, cemetery) are not justified by population at all, because the whole nation (10,000) is smaller than one kelurahan (30,000). Treat them as political choices: each city as a mini-kelurahan seat, plus one national kecamatan-level cluster in the capital.
- **Land:** the m²/person columns add up to roughly 9–10 m² of facility land per person at RT/RW level: 1.25 SD + 0.28 TK + 0.36 musholla + 0.24 masjid + 0.4 shop + 1.0 + 0.5 parks + 0.12 hall + 0.12 clinic + 0.048 posyandu + 0.09 reading room + small items. That is about 2 ha per 2,000-person city before secondary schools and kelurahan-level functions.

### Gaps
- The SNI has no hospital (rumah sakit) row and no cemetery land size. Hospital class ratios (type A/B/C/D, beds per population) are set by separate Ministry of Health rules, which I did not retrieve.
- I did not retrieve the current puskesmas rule (Permenkes 43/2019 is commonly cited as "at least 1 puskesmas per kecamatan") or the current PPDB zonasi / SPMB rule. They are not verified here.
- The SMU land figures in Tabel 11 are scrambled in the PDF layout. Confirm them against a scanned original before using them as exact.

## 2. Equivalent standards elsewhere (US, UK, Japan, Singapore, Germany)

### Takeaway
Other countries rarely publish one universal table like the SNI. They set hierarchies: Singapore town / neighbourhood / precinct and Germany Grund- / Mittel- / Oberzentrum. They also set distance or time rules: US fire response 240 s / 1.5 miles; Japanese school commute 4 km / 6 km. Some ratios are per-capita: US parks at 1 park per ~2,400 residents and ~10 acres per 1,000; UK at 1 GP per 1,800 patients.

### Cited Findings

**United States: fire**
- NFPA 1710 (career departments): the first engine company must arrive within 240 seconds of travel time on 90% of incidents. The initial full-alarm assignment must arrive within 480 seconds of travel time on 90% of incidents — [MTAS, Univ. of Tennessee](https://mtas.tennessee.edu/reference/estimating-travel-time-fire-apparatus); [IAFF NFPA 1710 summary](https://www.iaff.org/wp-content/uploads/English_Version_-_NFPA_1710_standards_DFSR_Summary_2022_new.pdf)
- ISO Fire Suppression Rating Schedule: full credit needs a first-due engine within 1.5 road miles and a ladder/service company within 2.5 road miles of built-up residential and commercial areas — [ISO Mitigation, "Criteria for deployment analysis of companies"](https://www.isomitigation.com/ppc/technical/criteria-for-deployment-analysis-of-companies/); [MTAS](https://mtas.tennessee.edu/reference/estimating-travel-time-fire-apparatus)
- ISO travel-time equation T = 0.65 + 1.7·D (minutes, miles). 1.5 miles comes out at about 3.2 min (192 s). Departments can instead prove arrival within 240 s on 90% of responses. By the RAND equation, 240 s equals about 1.97 miles — [MTAS](https://mtas.tennessee.edu/reference/estimating-travel-time-fire-apparatus)

**United States: parks**
- NRPA 2025 Agency Performance Review: the typical (median) agency has 1 park per 2,411 residents and 10.2 acres of parkland per 1,000 residents — [NRPA 2025 APR](https://archive22377.nrpa.org/globalassets/research/research-reports/2025-agency-performance-review-report.pdf)
- NRPA 2026 APR (June 2026): 1 park per 2,437 residents and about 10 acres per 1,000. The data is self-reported by agencies through Park Metrics — [NRPA 2026 APR](https://www.nrpa.org/globalassets/research/research-reports/2026-agency-performance-review.pdf); [news coverage](https://sportsfieldmanagementonline.com/2026/06/09/new-benchmarking-report-shows-one-park-per-2437-residents/19364/)

**United Kingdom: primary care**
- 1,800 people per full-time GP is treated as the standard by NHS London's Healthy Urban Development Unit (HUDU), as cited in a UK government fact sheet on new homes and healthcare — [GOV.UK Fact sheet 4](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/new-homes-fact-sheet-4-new-homes-and-healthcare-facilities/fact-sheet-4-new-homes-and-healthcare-facilities)
- Floor space of 0.085 m² per patient, from DH Health Building Note 11-01, used by BLMK ICB in 2025; build cost £7,334/m² (2025) — [Bedford / BLMK ICB s106 guidance](https://www.bedford.gov.uk/files/blmk-icb-s106-contributions-healtheffective-01.10.2025.pdf/download)
- Scotland (Edinburgh 2023): 1,500 patients per GP and 271 m² of floor area allowed per GP, from Scottish Health Planning Notes — [Edinburgh developer contributions appendix](https://consultationhub.edinburgh.gov.uk/sfc/developer-contributions-sup-guidance/user_uploads/pt-4-appendix-10-healthcare-contribution-zone-updated-maps-2.pdf)
- The HUDU planning contributions model is NHS software, used mainly in London, that converts new housing into a healthcare floorspace and cost need per dwelling or person. A London Plan HUDU model run dated 2026 exists — [London Datastore](https://data.london.gov.uk/dataset/london-plan-nhs-hudu-model-2026-2gq4w)

**Japan: schools and community centres**
- Standard school size is 12–18 classes for elementary schools (School Education Act Enforcement Regulations, Art. 41), applied to junior high schools by Art. 79. The standard is flexible: "not applicable where special circumstances exist" — [Iruma City school-size material](https://www.city.iruma.saitama.jp/material/files/group/11/shiryou1_gakkoukibo.pdf); [Izumisano legal excerpt](https://www.city.izumisano.lg.jp/material/files/group/7/03_hourei.pdf)
- "Appropriate school size" for national subsidies (施設費国庫負担法施行令, Art. 4): about 12–18 classes, and a commute of about 4 km or less for elementary school and about 6 km or less for junior high. Combined compulsory schools (義務教育学校) are 18–27 classes — [Iruma City](https://www.city.iruma.saitama.jp/material/files/group/11/shiryou1_gakkoukibo.pdf)
- MEXT 2015 guidance keeps 4 km / 6 km as a rough guide for walking and cycling, with about 1 hour as the travel-time guide — [Takahashi City](https://www.city.takahashi.lg.jp/uploaded/life/37903_118225_misc.pdf)
- Kominkan (公民館, community learning centre): 13,798 nationwide including similar facilities (MEXT Social Education Survey, FY2021), with numbers falling — [MEXT](https://www.mext.go.jp/a_menu/01_l/08052911/001.htm)
- Koban (police box) example for one prefecture: Osaka had 599 koban and 46 chuzaisho (residential police boxes) at the end of FY2021 — [Osaka Prefecture document](https://www.police.pref.osaka.lg.jp/material/files/group/2/r0805_koban_gaiyo.pdf). Nationally there were about 11,200 koban/chuzaisho liaison councils as of 1 April 2024. That counts councils, not boxes — [Police White Paper 2024](https://www.npa.go.jp/hakusyo/r06/honbun/html/aa2231000.html). There were about 8,500 chuzaisho in 1994 — [Police White Paper 1995](https://www.npa.go.jp/hakusyo/h07/h070201.html)

**Singapore: HDB new town hierarchy**
- "Typically, a town has around 25,000 to 50,000 dwelling units, while each neighbourhood has around 4,000 to 6,000 dwelling units. Within each neighbourhood are precincts of about 600 to 1,000 dwelling units each." Each town has a central hub serving several neighbourhoods. Neighbourhoods are served by precincts with kindergartens, shops and eateries — [CLC, "Singapore: Housing a Nation" essay](https://www.clc.gov.sg/docs/default-source/urban-solutions/urb-sol-iss-1-pdfs/essay-singapore-housing-a-nation.pdf)
- Placement by hierarchy: shopping complexes and hawker centres at the town centre; smaller supermarkets, mini-marts and eating houses in neighbourhoods and precincts; parks, commercial and educational facilities allocated per town, neighbourhood and precinct. Each town is "self-sufficient" (schools, shops, recreation) to cut trips downtown. Precinct-level facilities include three-generation playgrounds / fitness corners and precinct pavilions; the HDB void deck serves the block level — [CLC essay](https://www.clc.gov.sg/docs/default-source/urban-solutions/urb-sol-iss-1-pdfs/essay-singapore-housing-a-nation.pdf)
- Punggol (1990s) model: smaller estates of 2,000–3,000 households served by a shop cluster, usually at a transport node — [search summary of secondary sources incl. NUS CDE](https://cde.nus.edu.sg/nuscities/how-hdbs-neighbourhood-retail-is-evolving/) (secondary source)

**Germany: Zentrale-Orte system**
- Per the MKRO resolution, three tiers: Grundzentrum (basic supra-local supply), Mittelzentrum and Oberzentrum. Each higher tier also performs the lower tiers' functions. Central places must be able to serve beyond their own needs — [Saxony LEP materials](https://www.landesentwicklung.sachsen.de/download/Landesentwicklung/31_TB_ZentraleOrteRaumkategorien.pdf)
- Saxony (LEP 2013 and 2025 report): 5 Oberzentren plus 1 Oberzentral city association, 35 Mittelzentren plus 3 associations, 68 Grundzentren plus 16 associations. 161 of 418 municipalities have at least a basic central function. The LEP sets a 10,000-inhabitant mark for Mittelzentren. On 31 Dec 2024, 42.0% of residents lived in Oberzentren, 21.6% in Mittelzentren and 17.5% in Grundzentren (81.2% in some central place). The aim is at most 90 min to the nearest Oberzentrum — [Saxony state development report, 211_ZO.pdf](https://www.landesentwicklung.sachsen.de/download/211_ZO.pdf)
- Thresholds vary by state. Hessen: Grundzentren at least 3,000 inhabitants (regional plan), and a draft LEP sets Unterzentren "usually not below 5,000". Saxony: 16 Grundzentren had fewer than 7,000 people in their catchment at the end of 2024. Mittelzentren in agglomerations need about 3× the population of rural ones for the same facilities (Hessen analysis) — [RP Darmstadt](https://rp-darmstadt.hessen.de/sites/rp-darmstadt.hessen.de/files/2022-05/gnr_-_12._sitzung_am_03.03.2016.pdf); [Hessen LEP draft](https://landesplanung.hessen.de/sites/landesplanung.hessen.de/files/2022-08/4_LEP_Ae_2_Beteiligung_Plantext_201101_barr_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- A 2,000-person city is about the size of one or two Singapore precincts (600–1,000 dwelling units × ~3 people), or a small German Grundzentrum. It fits one US park (1 per ~2,400) and about 20 acres (8 ha) of parkland at NRPA's median.
- Fire: a 1.5-mile (2.4 km) engine radius covers about 18 km². A compact 2,000-person city needs at most 1 station, and the whole nation could be served by 1–5 stations depending on map spacing.
- GP: 2,000 people ÷ 1,800 per GP gives about 1 GP-equivalent per city. At 0.085 m² per patient that is about 170 m² of primary-care floor space per city.

### Gaps
- No primary source was retrieved for: US school size norms, US public library outlets per capita (IMLS), UK fire station standards (UK has no national response-time standard; not verified), UK school place planning (DfE pupil yield), the German facility lists per tier (Krankenhaus, Gymnasium, etc.), or the national count of Japanese koban. My unverified memory of roughly 6,000 koban and 6,000 chuzaisho should not be used without checking.
- NFPA 1710 staffing (minimum crew per engine) and turnout times were not confirmed in a primary source here.
- HDB facility-per-unit ratios (e.g., primary school per X units, polyclinic per town) were not found in an official HDB or URA document. The HDB Town Design Guides were not opened.

## 3. Healthcare: hospital beds and doctors per 1,000 people (by country)

### Takeaway
Beds per 1,000 range from 0.5 in Nigeria (very old data point) to about 12.6–12.8 in Japan and Korea. Doctors per 1,000 range from 0.38 (Nigeria) to 4.5 (Germany). For a 2,000-person city, the real-world range is about 1–26 beds and 1–9 doctors.

- The OECD average is about 4.2 beds per 1,000 (2023). Korea and Japan have 12–13 — [OECD Health at a Glance 2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/11/health-at-a-glance-2025_a894f72e/full-report/indicator-overview-country-dashboards-and-major-trends_368ac397.html)
- Clinic tiers that exist as standards: Indonesia posyandu (1,250) → balai pengobatan (2,500) → doctor's practice (5,000) → puskesmas pembantu / maternity clinic / pharmacy (30,000) → puskesmas (120,000) — [SNI Tabel 12](https://repository.unikom.ac.id/32520/1/SNI%2003-1733-2004%20Tata%20Cara%20Perenc.%20Lingk.%20Perumahan%20di%20Perkotaan.pdf). UK: GP practice at 1 GP per 1,800 — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/new-homes-fact-sheet-4-new-homes-and-healthcare-facilities/fact-sheet-4-new-homes-and-healthcare-facilities)

### Inferences
- For a 2,000-person city: Indonesia-level gives about 3 beds and 1 doctor; UK/US-level about 5 beds and 7 doctors; Japan/Korea-level about 25 beds and 5 doctors. A single small hospital of about 25–50 beds is realistic only at national scale (10,000 people → 14–126 beds depending on country model). Each city fits a clinic or GP practice.

### Gaps
- Nurses per 1,000 and hospital size classes (beds per hospital) were not retrieved.
- Nigeria's beds figure is from 2004 and should be treated as unreliable.

## 4. Education, local government buildings, religious buildings, cemeteries, sports

### Takeaway
Indonesia gives explicit per-population counts: SD per 1,600–2,000 residents; SMP/SMA per 4,800; TK per 1,250; reading room per 2,500. Japan defines a school by class count (12–18) and commute (4 / 6 km). Local government buildings scale with administrative level: RW meeting hall → kelurahan office (30,000) → kecamatan office, police and fire (120,000). Only Indonesia's SNI gives worship-building ratios. Cemetery land is not quantified in any source found.

### Inferences
- **Suggested building catalogue for COUNTRY AI.** These combine the sourced ratios above with a design choice to give each city a seat of government.
  - Per city (2,000 people):
    - City hall: kelurahan-office model, 500 m² floor / 1,000 m² land.
    - 1 community hall (balai warga).
    - 1 clinic (balai pengobatan) plus 2 posyandu-type health posts.
    - 2 kindergartens and 1 primary school.
    - 1 reading room / library.
    - 1 mosque or multi-faith hall plus several small prayer rooms.
    - 1 watch post (pos hansip / koban equivalent).
    - About 8 corner shops plus 1 small market.
    - 1 park of about 1,250 m² plus several playgrounds.
    - 1 waste collection point.
  - At national scale (10,000 people), in the capital or shared:
    - 1 SMP and 1 SMA, or a combined secondary school; Japan's 6 km commute and Indonesia's 3 km SMA radius both allow sharing.
    - 1 health centre (puskesmas) / small hospital of 14–126 beds depending on the country model.
    - 1 police station.
    - 1 fire station, or a fire post per city if cities are more than ~2.4 km apart.
    - 1 post office.
    - 1 multipurpose hall / assembly.
    - 1 cemetery.
    - 1 landfill / recycling depot.
    - National institutions with no real per-population standard: courthouse, parliament, academy / university, national library. These follow the simulation's institutions rather than population.
- Staff roles: no source here gives staffing standards per facility type, except GPs (1 per 1,800 patients) and school classes (40 pupils per class implied by the SNI school types). Any staff numbers for a map will need assumptions.

### Gaps
- No sourced per-population standard was found for: universities or vocational schools; civil registry offices; ambulance stations; churches or temples (outside Indonesia's "local custom"); cemetery land per person; US school size; UK school places; German facility lists.
- India, Brazil, Nigeria and South Korea were covered only for health ratios. Pupils-per-teacher and schools-per-population figures were not retrieved because the World Bank API timed out.
